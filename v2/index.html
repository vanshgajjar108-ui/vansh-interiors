02RESEARCH AIM

To systematically design, test, and articulate an adaptive interior spatial methodology whereby a singular fixed structural grid accommodates radically contrasting daily human routines—spanning compact urban living to multi-generational family routines and luxury configurations—without degrading functional spatial dignity or requiring structural demolitions.

03STUDIO OBJECTIVES
OBJ_01

PRECEDENT DECONSTRUCTION

Scrutinize international precedent models (modular and open-building typologies) to identify mechanisms of internal mobility.

OBJ_02

GRID METRICS ESTABLISHMENT

Formulate an uncompromising modular spatial framework capable of housing MEP utilities and spatial boundaries.

OBJ_03

TYPOLOGICAL OCCUPANT MAPPING

Formulate five distinct user behavioral profiles defining distinct spatial demands and circadian rhythms.

OBJ_04

ADAPTIVE GEOMETRY TESTING

Develop precise CAD plans, sections, and tectonic elevations resolving spatial boundaries for each individual scenario.

OBJ_05

EXTRACT GENERAL PRINCIPLES

Distill universal principles of interior architectural flexibility transferable across urban domestic typologies.

OBJ_06

COMPREHENSIVE DISSEMINATION

Produce an editorial thesis portfolio with technical drawings, spatial transformations, and analytical diagrams.

“Spatial design achieves authenticity when form recedes, allowing human life, quiet light, and honest materials to conduct the experience.”

I am an interior design student at Indus University, Ahmedabad, pursuing a research-led approach to interior architecture, residential environments, and spatial frameworks. My practice prioritizes how human rituals physically interact with volumes, circulation thresholds, and tactile materiality.

Rather than adopting fleeting ornamental trends, my academic trajectory explores how structural grids and modular discipline can construct spaces that endure. My thesis on adaptable domestic spaces synthesizes rigorous analysis of global precedents with human-centered empathy.

I seek opportunities to contribute to design studios, research teams, and graduate academic spaces that value integrity, spatial clarity, and intellectual rigor.
